The Lore

Before Dreamfall became a fractured world of dungeons, monsters, magic, and forgotten memories, two kingdoms fought over what the realm was meant to become.

Dreamfall

Long before the ruins, the dungeons, and the strange creatures that now wander the realm, Dreamfall was a peaceful and unusually magical place.

It was a world where the boundary between imagination and reality was thinner than anywhere else. Dreams could influence the physical world, memories could leave impressions on the places where they were made, and strong emotions could sometimes give shape to things that should have existed only in someone's mind.

Because of this, Dreamfall became a place of incredible beauty. Forests grew around ancient ruins, flowers bloomed in places where no seeds had been planted, and entire landscapes seemed to change depending on the dreams of the people who lived there.

But such a world could never remain simple.

Magic that could create beautiful things could also create dangerous ones. A forgotten nightmare could become a monster. A person's grief could linger in a building long after they were gone. A dream repeated often enough could eventually become something real.

The people of Dreamfall eventually learned to live with these strange rules.

Two civilizations became especially powerful: the Kingdom of Dreamers and the kingdoms of the Dungeon Dwellers beneath them.

For generations, the two peoples lived separately. They traded occasionally, shared resources when necessary, and largely stayed within their own worlds.

That peace would not last.

“Dreams were never meant to stay asleep.”

The Kingdom of Dreamers

The Kingdom of Dreamers occupied much of the surface world. Its cities were built around the idea that imagination was the greatest source of magic.

The Dreamers believed that anything could be created if someone could dream it strongly enough.

Their kingdom was filled with enchanted gardens, towering castles, strange little villages, floating structures, magical libraries, and markets where ordinary objects could possess extraordinary properties.

Their magic was deeply connected to emotion.

Happiness could strengthen a spell. Fear could distort one. Love could preserve memories. Anger could create destructive bursts of magic.

Because of this, Dreamer society placed enormous importance on controlling emotions and controlling magic.

Their rulers taught that the realm belonged to those capable of shaping it responsibly.

For a long time, this philosophy seemed to work.

The cities prospered, the kingdom expanded, and the Dreamers began believing they understood Dreamfall better than anyone else.

Eventually, that belief became arrogance.

The Dreamers began looking beneath the surface with increasing suspicion.

They knew the Dungeon Dwellers possessed ancient magic. They knew the dungeons contained artifacts older than either kingdom.

And they became afraid of what might happen if that power was ever brought to the surface.

The Dungeon Dwellers

Beneath Dreamfall lies a second world.

Deep beneath the forests and kingdoms of the surface are enormous caverns, underground cities, forgotten temples, labyrinths, and dungeons that seem to stretch farther than anyone has ever been able to map.

This is where the Dungeon Dwellers made their home.

The name “Dungeon Dweller” became a common term among the surface kingdoms, but the people living below never thought of themselves as a single race.

The underground kingdoms were home to countless species and cultures.

Some Dwellers resembled humans. Others looked like creatures from nightmares. Some were covered in fur, scales, feathers, or strange magical markings.

Their differences did not make them less civilized.

They had families, governments, religions, merchants, artists, scholars, warriors, farmers, and entire cities hidden beneath the surface.

Their relationship with magic was also different from the Dreamers.

Instead of shaping magic through dreams and emotion, many Dwellers learned to manipulate the ancient magic already buried within the world.

The deeper a dungeon went, the older the magic became.

The Dwellers believed that the surface kingdoms were slowly destroying something they did not understand.

They had watched the Dreamers expand their cities, build over ancient ruins, and take artifacts from places that had belonged to the underground kingdoms for centuries.

To the Dwellers, the Dreamers were not protecting Dreamfall.

They were claiming it.

“They called us monsters because they had never bothered to learn our names.”

The War Between Kingdoms

The exact beginning of the war has been lost.

Every surviving account tells a slightly different story.

Some records claim the Dungeon Dwellers attacked a surface settlement without warning.

Other records claim the Dreamers entered an ancient dungeon and took something that was never theirs to take.

Some historians believe the conflict had been building for generations before either side finally raised an army.

Whatever the truth was, the first battle changed everything.

The conflict spread quickly.

Surface cities were attacked from below.

Dungeon kingdoms were invaded from above.

Entire settlements were caught between the two sides.

Creatures that had never belonged to either kingdom were forced to choose sides.

Magic became a weapon.

Dreams were turned into soldiers.

Ancient dungeon creatures were released onto the surface.

Neither kingdom was innocent.

As the war continued, the original reason for fighting became less important than simply surviving it.

Children were born knowing only war.

Villages disappeared.

Ancient knowledge was destroyed.

And eventually, both kingdoms began using magic that neither side completely understood.

The Fracture

The final days of the war are the least understood part of Dreamfall's history.

Both kingdoms had exhausted nearly everything they had.

The Dreamers had begun using increasingly powerful dream magic, while the Dungeon Dwellers opened deeper and deeper parts of the ancient dungeons in search of power.

Something was found.

What it was has been lost to history.

Some believe it was an ancient source of magic.

Others believe it was something alive.

A few surviving texts suggest that the object was neither a weapon nor a source of power, but a doorway.

Whatever the truth may be, the magic unleashed during the final battle was unlike anything Dreamfall had experienced before.

The sky changed.

The earth shifted.

Dreams spilled into the waking world.

Memories became places.

Places began remembering people who had never lived there.

Entire sections of the realm seemed to disappear and reappear somewhere else.

The boundaries between the surface world, the dungeons, dreams, and reality began collapsing.

This became known as the Fracture.

The war effectively ended that day.

But Dreamfall itself never recovered.

“The war ended. The world didn't.”

The Star Inn

After the Fracture, strange places began appearing throughout Dreamfall.

Some were temporary.

Others remained for years.

The strangest of these places is the Star Inn.

The inn appears to be an ordinary building from the outside. A cozy place with warm lights, comfortable rooms, food, and enough space for travelers to rest.

But the inside of the Star Inn follows different rules.

Violence cannot occur there.

Weapons refuse to function.

Spells designed to harm another person simply fail.

Even creatures with violent instincts seem to lose the ability to act upon them while inside.

Nobody knows who created the enchantment.

Some believe the inn existed before the war.

Others believe it appeared because of the Fracture.

The strangest part is that the Star Inn welcomes everyone.

Dreamers.

Dungeon Dwellers.

Adventurers.

Monsters.

And people who no longer remember what they are.

The Portal

Somewhere beyond the Star Inn is a strange portal.

Nobody remembers building it.

Nobody knows where it leads.

The portal is one-way.

Travelers can enter.

They cannot return through the same doorway.

Some believe it leads into the deepest parts of the old dungeon kingdoms.

Others believe it leads to places created by the Fracture itself.

A few believe the portal leads outside Dreamfall.

Nobody has been able to prove any of these theories.

What makes the portal particularly strange is that it does not appear to behave the same way for everyone.

Some travelers claim to have seen different doors.

Others swear the portal whispered their name.

Mylo refuses to discuss it.

After the War

Generations have passed since the war.

The kingdoms that once ruled Dreamfall no longer exist in the forms they once did.

Their castles have become ruins.

Their armies have disappeared.

Their borders have been swallowed by forests, dungeons, and shifting landscapes.

Most people alive today were born long after the war.

They know it through stories passed down by parents, books written by historians, old songs, broken monuments, and warnings scratched into dungeon walls.

But history has become unreliable.

A Dreamer may tell you that the Dungeon Dwellers began the war.

A Dungeon Dweller may tell you that the Dreamers started it.

An old ruin might tell you something completely different.

There are even places where the past appears to repeat itself.

A traveler may walk through a ruined village and suddenly see it as it looked decades ago.

They may hear conversations between people who died long ago.

They may even meet someone who should no longer exist.

In Dreamfall, the past is never quite as dead as it should be.

What Was Lost

Nobody truly knows how the war began.

Nobody knows exactly what happened during the final battle.

Nobody knows what caused the Fracture.

Nobody knows what happened to the rulers of either kingdom.

And nobody knows what was found beneath the deepest dungeon before the world changed.

The surviving records contradict one another.

Ancient books tell different stories than the ruins themselves.

Statues have been found depicting people who do not appear in any historical record.

Some dungeons seem to have been built before either kingdom existed.

And some of the oldest creatures in Dreamfall seem to remember a time before the kingdoms ever existed.

There are people who know the truth.

Or at least pieces of it.

Mylo is one of them.

He remembers the world before the war.

He remembers the war itself.

And he remembers what happened afterward.

But Mylo has never told Nessa everything.

Perhaps he is protecting her from the truth.

Or perhaps he is protecting the truth from her.

“Every dungeon has a bottom. Every dream has an end. The question is what happens when you reach either.”
There is always another door.
